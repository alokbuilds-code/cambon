THE HOUSE

“Fashion fades, only style remains the same.” An unyielding commitment to Parisian discipline, geometric liberation, and timeless architecture.

HERITAGE · 31 RUE CAMBON

THE MIRRORED STAIRCASE

In 1918, Gabrielle Chanel acquired the townhouse at 31 Rue Cambon, establishing a radical epicenter where the boutique, the haute couture salons, the private apartment, and the creative ateliers coalesced under one roof.

From the legendary mirrored art deco staircase, Mademoiselle sat invisibly on the fifth step, observing the reactions of society as models descended. Every surface reflected light at deliberate angles—a visual geometry that inspired the faceted flacon of N°5 and the octagonal architecture of the Place Vendôme watch cases.

AESTHETIC PHILOSOPHY

MONOCHROME PURITY

Before Chanel, black was reserved solely for mourning and uniform workwear. By elevating noir to the supreme expression of feminine sovereignty, she stripped away decorative ornament in favor of architectural silhouette.

White illuminated the face through pearls and camellias; beige evoked the sunlit sand of the French coast; black anchored the gaze with uncompromising authority.

01

FREEDOM OF MOVEMENT

Jackets constructed without stiff interlinings, sleeves cut high at the armhole, and skirt hemlines weighted with subtle chains so the garment moves fluidly with the human body.

02

MÉTIERS D'ART

Preserving the rare historic crafts of Parisian embroidery, feathered plume work, goldsmithing, and shoe making through generational master workshops.

03

TIMELESS GEOMETRY

Diamond quilting, the square flacon, the octagonal bezel—pure forms that exist outside seasonal trends and resist temporal decay.
